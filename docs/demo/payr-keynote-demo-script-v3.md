# PayR keynote demo script — final narrative draft

> Preserved root donor, 10 September reconciliation. [Current Recording Scope](README.md#current-recording-scope-10-september) supersedes publication/email and unverified universal-client/logo/accounting claims below. This narrative draft is not live evidence or the final approved recording.

**Target runtime:** 3 minutes 30 seconds
**Speaker:** Keng
**Style:** Personal keynote followed by a live product story
**Core line:** Any AI agent that can call tools can use PayR.

## 0:00–0:18 | Hook

**Screen**

Keng speaks directly to camera. Keep the background clean. Do not show a title slide yet.

**Dialogue**

> Invoicing is tedious.
>
> As a software developer, billing my clients takes far longer than it should. The work may be finished, but getting paid is still a long process.
>
> That is why we built PayR.

**Screen**

Cut to the PayR logo.

> Invoicing. Simplified.

## 0:18–0:42 | The problem behind the payment

**Screen**

Show each step appearing as Keng names it:

`Find client details → Create invoice → Send instructions → Check payment → Match transfer → Create receipt`

**Dialogue**

> Before I can send an invoice, I need to collect all these details manually, send payment instructions, monitor my wallet, match the transfer to the invoice, and create a receipt.
>
> USDC can move in seconds. Everything around the payment can still take hours.

## 0:42–1:02 | The PayR idea

**Screen**

Collapse the long workflow into:

`Ask → Confirm → Pay → Done`

**Dialogue**

> PayR turns the AI agent you already use into an invoicing and payment assistant.
>
> You describe the work once. PayR helps complete the invoice, gives the client one clear way to pay, verifies the transaction, and handles the receipt.

## 1:02–1:20 | Works with tool-calling agents

**Screen**

Show Claude, Claude Codex, Hermes, OpenClaw, and other relevant agent logos around the PayR logo. Add a small caption:

> For AI agents that support tool calls

**Dialogue**

> PayR works out of the box with the agents people already use.
>
> Any AI agent that can call tools can use PayR. The agent handles the conversation, while PayR handles the invoice and payment workflow.

## 1:20–1:50 | Create the invoice

**Screen**

Open the demonstrated AI agent and type:

> Invoice Circle 1 testnet USDC for the September API milestone, due Friday.

Show the agent calling PayR and returning the completed draft.

**Dialogue**

> I give my agent four things: the client, the work, the amount, and the due date. We are using one testnet USDC for this demonstration.
>
> The agent can use an existing PayR profile, its memory, or public information to help assemble the details. PayR checks the required fields and gives me one complete draft to confirm.

**Screen details to highlight**

- Sender and client
- Company address and registration details
- Service description
- Due date
- Payout wallet
- Exact USDC amount

## 1:50–2:10 | Human approval

**Screen**

Hold on the draft. Show Keng reviewing the terms and approving publication.

**Dialogue**

> I do not need to retype every field, but I still stay in control. I review the business details, amount, due date, and wallet.
>
> Only after I approve does PayR finalize the invoice and create its private payment link.

## 2:10–2:30 | Invoice delivery

**Screen**

Show PayR sending the invoice email. Switch to the client inbox and open the real delivered message. Show the invoice PDF and payment button. Then briefly show the freelancer's copy.

**Dialogue**

> PayR sends the finalized invoice and payment link to my client, with a copy for me.
>
> The client receives everything in one place: what the payment is for, who receives it, the exact amount, and the correct network.

## 2:30–2:54 | Client payment

**Screen**

Open the payment link in the client browser. Connect the pre-funded wallet and approve the real Arc testnet transaction. Cut the confirmation wait, then show the Arc explorer transaction.

**Dialogue**

> The client reviews the invoice and pays from their own wallet. PayR never controls their money.
>
> The contract checks the payment terms, rejects the wrong amount or a duplicate payment, and sends the USDC directly to me.

## 2:54–3:30 | Automatic confirmation and close

**Screen**

Return to PayR. Show the invoice changing from `Published` to `Paid`. Open the confirmation receipt email and place it beside the matching Arc explorer transaction. End on the PayR logo.

**Dialogue**

> Once payment succeeds, PayR verifies the transaction on Arc. The invoice changes to Paid, and both sides receive a confirmation receipt with the payment details and transaction proof.
>
> I spend less time on administration. My client gets a simpler payment experience. And our accounting records agree automatically.
>
> The work is complete. The payment is proven. The paperwork is done.
>
> That is PayR.

## Recording notes

- Use the agent client that produces the cleanest verified demo. The logo scene communicates the tool-calling model; the live scene proves one real integration.
- Make every business detail fictional or explicitly approved for display.
- The agent may propose details from memory or public search, but the video must show Keng confirming them before publication.
- Record real invoice and receipt emails after delivery is complete. Do not build fake inbox footage.
- Use a fresh invoice and a pre-funded wallet. Do not repay the preserved evidence invoice.
- Show the Arc transaction or event clearly enough for judges to verify it.
- Cut transaction waiting time during editing, but keep the actual transaction hash and resulting Paid state.
- Keep financing out of the main demo. If needed, mention it after the product proof as a future use of verified payment history.

> PayR
>
> Invoices. Paid. Powered.
>
> Private invoices. Verifiable payments.
